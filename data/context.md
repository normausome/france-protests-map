# France unrest, Sept–Oct 2026: context and official figures
Compiled Mon 19 Oct 2026, about 10:00 AM ET (16:00 Paris). Figures move quickly, so each item below carries its source and date. Times are Paris time (CEST, UTC+2) unless marked otherwise.

## What the protests are about
- **Origin: a high-school (lycéen) movement.** It started on 17–18 Sept 2026 at Lycée Saint-Exupéry in Créteil over teacher shortages, run-down schools and Parcoursup. It went national from 28 Sept. Sources: fr.wikipedia "Mouvement lycéen et étudiant de 2026 en France" (secondary source); Guardian, 17 Oct (https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/17/protests-france-high-prices-social-security-gilets-jaunes); BBC, 18 Oct (https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c574pdzl0k4no).
- **Broadening to a cost-of-living revolt.** It merged with anger over the cost of living and fuel prices, driven by former gilets jaunes and backed by the CGT. The national day of action was Sat 17 Oct. About 550,000 marched according to the Interior Ministry (110,000 in Paris); the CGT says 800,000. The marches were mostly peaceful. On the fringes, violent groups and opportunistic looters attacked town halls, prefectures and thousands of shops.
- **18–19 Oct.** New looting and clashes took place mainly in Paris (République / Faubourg-du-Temple), Lyon and Marseille. On 19 Oct, students blockaded the Louvre and it closed. Sources: franceinfo 18 Oct; BFMTV 19 Oct; BBC live 19 Oct (https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c893v4gx2e47t).
- **Government response and what comes next (19 Oct):**
  - PM Lecornu authorised localised curfews and said a state of emergency is possible.
  - 200 investigators are reviewing footage.
  - Interior Minister Nuñez offered to resign; Macron refused.
  - Marseille banned gatherings on 19 Oct from 12:00 to 20:00.
  - The gilets jaunes have called an "Acte II" for Sat 24 Oct.
  - The Bordeaux and Toulouse prefectures also cite 5 Nov.
  - Sources: BBC live 19 Oct; NBC 19 Oct (https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/france-violence-riots-looting-paris-limoges-curfews-macron-protests-rcna604292).

## Main cities and regions with damage (17–18 Oct)
- **Worst hit:** Paris (République, bd Magenta, Strasbourg-Saint-Denis, Capucines, Madeleine/Faubourg-Saint-Honoré, Les Halles), Marseille, Lyon, Limoges, Bordeaux, Lille.
- **Heavy damage:** Grenoble, Rennes, Toulouse, Nantes, Montpellier, Rouen, Tours, Brest, Strasbourg, Saint-Étienne, Clermont-Ferrand.
- **Lesser damage:** Angers, Avignon, Valence, Quimper, Mulhouse, Pau, Bayonne, Elbeuf, Joué-lès-Tours.

## Notes and caveats
- The dataset covers incidents named or located by sources. The true number of damaged businesses (about 2,000–3,000) is far larger than the 151 rows.
- Some figures conflict as counts are updated. Use the latest consolidated figure (2,397 / 997) for national totals.
- Officials blame "ultra-left" groups for the violence. Mélenchon (LFI) has disputed this. These are political claims, not established facts.
- **Data gaps:**
  - Le Parisien is paywalled.
  - Le Populaire was only partly readable.
  - The Ouest-France Angers article was truncated.
  - 36 of the 151 incidents are geocoded only to the city centroid.
  - Some incidents are attempted break-ins rather than completed ones.
  - Severity for the La Barrique row is uncertain.
  - BFMTV's list of Paris shops hit on 18 Oct may mix in 17 Oct incidents, so only the appliance-store row was kept.
